# The Universe screen

What a person has read, shown as places: regions on planets, planets round stars (systems), and rooms as stations beside their star. This folder is the screen for your own Universe (`universe-screen.tsx`, the route `app/(tabs)/universe.tsx`). A friend's Universe (`features/visit/`) mounts the same surface over a session of its own. The behaviour list, routes and decisions are in `apps/client/README.md`; this file is the map of the folder.

## How a place becomes something drawn

1. **The session** (`universe-session.ts`, made per account by `use-universe-session.ts`) reads the person's scene from the server through `@knowscroll/client-core`: the sky, the manifest, chunks of systems and planets, geography. The server places everything once; nothing here moves a place. The session also holds where you are: `focusId` (nothing, a system, a planet or a region), `landed`, and `frame` (a pan or turn in screen points).
2. **The layout** (`universe-layout.ts`, `universe-places.ts`) turns the session's state into a view (`UniverseView`: the whole universe, a system, a planet) and an index of places.
3. **The surface** (`universe-surface.tsx`) is the chrome over the drawing: the header and Back pill, the region card, Lately, the list (`dial/` on a phone, Browse on a desktop), search, sheets. It picks the stage.
4. **The stage** draws the view:
   - `universe-stage.tsx`: the map as it ships, the 3D map and system (`space/`, three.js written by hand), a planet's globe and landed map as SVG (`planet/`), and the animated steps between them (`animation/`).
   - `voyage/`: the Round 7.3 look on trial, behind "Try the new Universe" (`universe-trial.ts`). One camera flies along a ladder of stops over the same three.js scene; a planet is one textured sphere from orbit to the ground; a ship the person can drag. See "The ladder" below.
   - `crossing/`: the film of the crossing to a friend's Universe (Round 7.3): one pure function of film time (`crossing-frame.ts`), one fragment shader in a lazily loaded three.js pass, and the 2D marks over it. `crossing-director.ts` is the pure state machine a screen plays it with (when it stops, when the names in common are fixed, skip, reduced motion, what is announced). A visit plays it behind the device switch (`features/visit/README.md`, `visit-crossing.tsx`), and then the camera falls into the friend's Universe from seven times out (`voyage/fall-in.ts`, the driver's `fallIn`); stills are at `/gallery-space?scene=crossing&q=0.30` (see "Looking at it locally").
   - `simple/`: the plain lists, when 3D is off (Simple drawing), fails, or the device is short of memory. Always kept: it is also how a screen reader reaches every place.
5. **Taps are buttons, not raycasts.** Every place on screen is a named button of at least 48 points laid over the canvas at the place's projected point; the canvas is hidden from assistive technology. Land not reached yet is drawn grey and is never named, counted, tapped or read out. In the trial look the names are not the only way to a region with a finger: one hidden press layer under them (planet stop and ground, `voyage-stage.tsx`) works out which *opened* region's land is under a tap from the last frame's `regionAt` and focuses it as its name would, so a tap that misses the name still lands on the region; the sea and land not reached answer no region, and it is no new focusable or named element.

## The ladder (the trial look)

`voyage/ladder.ts` holds the stops (`'outer' | 'universe' | 'star' | 'planet' | 'ground'`), what each stop shows (`STOPS`) and how a view maps onto one (`stopOf`, `targetOf`). The stored camera did not change: the stop follows from `focusId` and `landed`.

**The outer stop: past the rim, among your friends' universes.** The top of the ladder is the whole universe seen 3.3 times as far (`OUTER_SCALE` in `voyage-chains.ts`, on the universe's own link, so a flight between the two is an ordinary flight on one chain). It has no place and no data of its own: `stopOf(view, outer)` and `targetOf(view, outer)` say `outer` only for the whole universe with the flag set, and `view.kind` stays `'universe'`.
- **Nothing is stored.** The flag is a tiny in-memory store (`voyage/outer/outer-stop.ts`, `createOuterStop`) made once per signed-in Universe screen (`SignedInUniverse`) and handed to the tree by `OuterStopContext`. It is not in `universe.view.v1`, not in `scene-return.v1`, not in the session. With the switch off the provider holds null and no store is read; a friend's Universe (the visit) and the gallery have no provider, so they have no outer stop at all.
- **Reaching it.** At the universe stop a pinch out let go, a wheel notch out or `-` calls `enter()` instead of climbing (`voyage-controller.ts`; this also stops the junk Back step that `session.up()` at the root pushed). The camera flies out; `useOuterFriends` starts reading the roster when the flight starts (the Friends list's own paged read, `friend-pages.v2`; nothing is read per friend, and the answer is dropped on leaving).
- **Leaving it.** A pinch in, a wheel notch in, `+`, `=`, the Back pill "Your universe" (`universe-up`), Escape, the browser's or hardware Back, all call `leave()`: never a star. Pinch out, a wheel notch out and `-` do nothing and the gesture line says so. The composite Back (`backWithOuter` in `universe-screen.tsx`) goes list, then stop, then the session's own; the web's history marker follows its `canBack`. The flag is also dropped when the screen blurs or the app goes to the background (a visit pushes `/visit/<handle>`, which blurs the tab, so Leave on it lands on your universe) and whenever `focusId` is not null. The blur leaves the stop in a microtask, after the rest of the blur has run: Back's own cleanup must stop listening first, or the web's history marker sees Back become impossible, goes back and takes the visit with it (found by the browser spec).
- **What is drawn.** The driver reports where the universe is on screen (`VoyageFrame.universe`: centre with the lean, radius, and `fade`, which is `smooth(1.15, 2.1, distance / the universe stop's distance)`), so the friends come in as a pinch spreads past the universe, stay through the flight, and go as it comes back. `voyage-controller.ts` tells the stage only when the centre moves 0.5 pt or `fade` 0.01, and never while `fade` is 0; `outer/outer-field.tsx` reads that with `useSyncExternalStore` and draws `outer/outer-layer.tsx` over the canvas. The header (`universe-header.tsx`) says "Beyond your universe" and the line ("14 friends", `outer-copy.ts`); the chips row is the single chip "All friends (14)" (`all-friends-list.tsx`), which opens the list of every friend in a sheet outside the stage. Friend buttons exist only once the camera is at the stop (`fade` at least 0.6), so a flight or a half-made pinch never starts a film; a friend's button is `router.push('/visit/<handle>')` and the crossing film is the visit's own.
- **Panning the field** moves the camera's aim (`control.lean`), so the 3D universe and the layer move together: a one-finger drag on the sky moves it one to one, the arrows 60 pt a press (← slides the field right, as looking round), and the ship's lean counts three times as much (`OUTER_LEAN_GAIN`, eased in over 0.3 s). The pan is held where five friends still show (`clampOuterPan` → `clampPan`) and starts from rest each time the stop is entered. The ship never counts a friend as a place to go to, and parks off the bubbles (`setObstacles`).
- **Browser spec:** `e2e/universe-outer-smoke.spec.ts`.

The camera driver (`voyage-driver.ts`) is loaded on demand with three.js (`voyage-loader.ts`). Its parts:

- `voyage-chains.ts` (pure): for a target, a chain of links, one per place it is in (planet, star, the whole universe), each a point and a distance.
- `flight.ts` (pure): how a flight moves between two chains.
- `voyage-view.ts`: which way the camera looks and where it stands.
- `planet-close-up.ts`: the planet as one world (`globe.ts`, painted by `world-texture.ts`), with a sharp patch of the map round the place you land.
- `ship-motion.ts` (pure) and `ship-layer.ts`: the ship. `ship-gravity.ts` (pure): how stars and planets pull it, and when one let go near it draws it in.
- `ship-ground.ts`, `ground-home.ts` and `ground-hit.ts` (pure): the ship on the ground, where it is always landed. `ship-ground.ts` is the lift (0.2 s up, 0.3 s to settle, eased, the same at any frame rate; none under reduced motion), the flame that follows it and the soft shadow; `ground-home.ts` is where it stands (on its region while that is in the ship's room, else at its spot, with hysteresis) and what the "you are here" pill says; `ground-hit.ts` is the opened region under a point of the globe, never closed land (D-109). The driver hands the controller `regionAt` for it wherever the globe is drawn (the planet stop as well as the ground), and the controller tells the stage through `onStands`; `bare-tap.ts` (pure) says what a tap that missed every name does with that answer.
- `sky-lens.ts` (pure), `sky-bend.ts` and `sky-pass.ts`: the look of gravity. Bodies are never bent; only the background behind them is. A solar system bends space as one mass round its whole extent (its planets' orbits, measured at its sun's depth), centred on its sun; a star alone bends round its glow; a planet bends space only when it is alone at its own planet stop, round its globe, never beside its sun; nothing bends on the ground. The background (the star shells and a faint deep field of 40,000 stars, the trial's only) is on its own three.js layer; the near motes stay with the bodies, unbent (`BACKGROUND_LAYER`, `space/scene/sky.ts`), moved there when the driver attaches (`BuiltSpace.splitSky()`). The driver's `drawScene` step (`space/space-renderer.ts`) draws that layer alone into a render target the size of the drawing buffer, draws the target to the screen in one full-screen pass bent round up to six lenses, then draws the rest of the scene unbent over it, then the ship. The pass reads the background at `c + d · (1 − θE² / max(r, R)²)` for a pixel at offset `d` from a lens (R the group's radius, θE = 0.9·R, the shift faded out between 3R and 6R, shifts added): outside R the sky is stretched into arcs round the mass, inside R it is an even zoom, and nothing inside a group is mirrored. The render target is multisampled like the screen, so with every θE at 0 the pass draws what a direct draw of the sky would, within 1 per channel almost everywhere (a probe at a planet stop found a few pixels on the globe's halo that differ, likely draw order). The deep field also holds 12,000 faint far smudges (soft ovals, each turned its own way: `SMUDGE_VERTEX` in `space/scene/shaders.ts`), which a bend stretches into the arcs the eye reads. `sky-lens.ts` holds that profile as `sourceOf`, a tested mirror of the shader. Lens weights ease from frame to frame, so a lens fades in and out; the still frame under reduced motion is bent the same way. The shader measures from the bottom of the view; the pass takes layout points, y down, and turns them. The layer split and the pass exist only once the voyage driver attaches: the shipping map draws its one pass as ever.
- `station-look.ts` (pure) and `station-layer.tsx`: a room's station, drawn flat over the canvas at its anchor in place of the scene's own mesh (`showStations`, called by the driver on attach), with the prototype's lights, glow and hover.
- `stand-apart.ts` (pure) and `stand-apart-scene.ts`: stations and forming dust stand apart in the trial look. A station is never drawn over or touching a sun's disc (nor another station), and a cloud of forming dust never over a sun's or a station's, with a 12-point gap. Each frame, after the camera is placed, the driver measures the suns, stations and clouds on screen, the solver says how far each must slide (along the line from what it overlaps, a continuous function of the camera), and each object is put at the place it was built at plus that slide in the camera's plane, so its depth and size do not change and the station's picture, button, name, the dust and the ship's drop target all read the moved place. Nothing is stored and the shared `placeStation` and `placeDust` are untouched. `STATION_CLEARS` in `stand-apart-scene.ts` is `'sun'` (clear the sun's disc) or `'group'` (clear the whole system out to its farthest planet's orbit, as `sky-bend.ts` measures it).

Search: the Back row of a star, a planet and its ground carries a small search circle (`universe-header.tsx`), and while the card is open the stage draws the sky's answer (`search-sky.tsx`): a dim, with a glow and a ring on each system the card reports as holding a match (`universe-search.ts` `systemsOfResults`, handed up by the card's `onLit`). The screen skips its flat dim in this look. `search-sky.tsx` is drawn from the overlay's anchors, over the canvas and under the buttons' touches.

The screen skips its flat dim in this look. `search-sky.tsx` is drawn from the overlay's anchors, over the canvas and under the buttons' touches.

Small finishing in this look (Round 7.3, #811): the List view chip and the Lately chip while off wear a dark glass fill (`SpaceChip`'s `glass`, passed only when the trial is on, so the old look's ghost chip is as it was). With Lately on, `calmNames` (`calm-names.ts`) marks the quiet stars and planets whose written names the overlay dims to 0.7, as the old overlay's `MapLabel calm` does; a region's name is never dimmed. "Return to X" (`return-chip.ts`, `returnOffered`) is not offered at the star that holds the parked planet: Back from a planet lands there. `UniverseStage` hands `VoyageStage` the same `onRefused` as the old stage, so a planet whose world cannot be drawn (`useGlobeWorld` refuses it) falls back to its plain list of regions. All four are tested through the whole screen in `universe-leftovers.test.tsx`, each with the switch off as well.

What we have in common: on a friend's whole-universe stop the visit hands the surface a chip and the shared systems' ids (`VisitorChrome.inCommon`, built in `features/visit/`), and the surface passes the sky `{ lit, shows: 'common' }`. The stage draws `CommonSky` under the names (25 % dim, a yellow ring of radius 9) and sets the names of systems that are not shared to 0.4. Search takes the sky over while its card is open.

The stage (`voyage-stage.tsx`) and its controller (`voyage-controller.ts`, gestures and the ship, outside React) reach the main bundle, so they never import three.js; `voyage-control.ts` is the typed channel between them and the driver. The controller's pure parts: `stage-touches.ts` (what the fingers mean, each followed by its id), `pinch-gesture.ts` (a pinch that follows the fingers and moves one stop) and `flight-legs.ts` (each flight timed for the frame-rate readout).

**Sizes.** The new look has no board of its own for a tablet: the product's breakpoints decide (`useDisplay`, 1024 px). A tablet (600 to 1023) takes the phone layout beside the shell's rail; from 1024 px the desktop one. Only the desktop changes the look, and only through what `UniverseSurface` hands down: `desktop` (the ship parks at 0.8 of the height, leans from 0.6 and may go from y 70 to H - 60: `SHIP_LAYOUT` in `voyage-controller.ts`, the prototype's `DESK` numbers), key hints that name the keys the new look listens to (`KeyHints`, a column bottom right), and no zoom buttons (`+` and `-` move one stop, as the wheel and Back do). The planet's caption (phone and tablet layouts) stands under the globe (`captionBelowGlobe`: the globe's bottom edge as the camera places it, plus the prototype's 30, never above 0.62 of the height, which is where it stood on a 390 x 844 phone). That reads the camera; nothing here moves it. The frame-rate readout, in this look, stands in the column under the header (`FrameRateFigure`), so it covers none of the header at any size; the old look keeps its corner. **Known gaps:** on the smallest phone (320 x 568) the chips and a notice leave the caption no room under the globe, so it stands on the chips as ever and meets the globe (a fix needs the camera to hold a smaller globe on a short screen, which a trial of this size did not take on: an attempt jumped the camera when the chips changed); not built: the prototype's rail and its search card at the left, and `AY = 0.5 H` for every stop on a desktop. W A S D fly the ship from the keyboard (`SHIP_KEYS` in `voyage-controller.ts`), so every gesture has a key.

**Adding a level** (a galaxy between the whole universe and a star): one more member of `Stop` and one row of `STOPS`; one more link in `chainOf`, after the star's and before the universe's (the camera reads a planet's chain as the planet's link, then the star's, whatever follows); `targetOf` and `stopOf` for the view that opens it; and the session's kind of place. A level with no data yet (a universe with no galaxy) is simply absent from a chain.

**The maps of a planet (the trial look).** `voyage/maps/` holds two screens that cover the stage: `PlanetMapScreen` (map 1), the whole planet as a flat SVG of the ground's own world with the formed regions named in colour, land not reached as ONE grey path with no name, count or press (D-109), and the list of formed regions under it ("4 regions", never "N of M"); and `RegionMapScreen` (map 2), the same drawing cropped, seam-aware, round one region, with the region's next Scroll or Reel and its other format with Open, and "you were here". Both take the planet, its formed regions, the looks and callbacks as props and read nothing; `universe-surface.tsx` wires them to the session. They exist only with the switch on, on the person's own landed planet with 3D drawn and at least one formed region; never in a friend's universe, in Simple drawing or on a globe.

- **How they open.** Map 1: the chip "Map of <planet>" in the chips row at the ground (`universe-map-open`, the button a keyboard and a screen reader reach), the `M` key on the focused stage (`voyage-controller.ts`, one more line in the desktop key hints), and a tap on bare ground (`voyage-ground-press` in `voyage-stage.tsx`: one invisible press layer under every name, neither named nor a tab stop; a press within 350 ms of a gesture is its end, not a tap, `justGestured`). Bare ground is the sea and land not reached: a tap on an opened region's land focuses that region instead, as its name does (`bare-tap.ts` decides, `controller.tapBare` reads the point, so the answer is the same at the planet stop, where the same layer is `voyage-globe-press` and a tap on the globe that is on no opened region lands on the planet, the "tap to land" the planet's hint says; the sky round the globe does nothing). All three call `session.showOverlay('planet-map')`. Map 2: a region's row or cell on map 1, and a region's name anywhere (the ground, a globe, search, the ship let go over it) through `session.focus(id)`, which opens the overlay `'place'`; with the maps drawn that overlay is map 2 and the old region card is not mounted (Simple drawing and the switch off keep the card).
- **Back is one step.** `universe-session.ts` knows the maps: `mapOrigin` (the camera map 1 was opened from), `state.mapBehind` and `mapPushed` (map 2 was reached from map 1, and whether that step added a Back step: sibling regions add none), `coveredMap` (Search or Browse opened over a map returns to it). Map 2 → map 1 → the camera map 1 was opened from; Escape, the header's Back chip, hardware Back and the browser's Back all call the session, so they agree. From the ground a name goes to map 2 and Back leaves the camera over that region, as Back from the card did. Opening a map writes and sends nothing; map 2 does the one preview read the card does; `focus(region)` records the device's own camera as ever. `showOverlay('planet-map')` is refused for a friend's session, as `'place'` is. The two publishes of a step reach listeners as one (`asOneStep`), so the browser's Back marker is never dropped between them.
- **What is under a map.** The stage stays mounted (a scene is costly to build again) and is out of reach: `inert` on the web (set through a ref in `universe-stage.tsx`), `importantForAccessibility="no-hide-descendants"` and `accessibilityElementsHidden` on native. The header, caption, chips and key hints are not drawn while a map is open. Focus moves to the map's title; closing map 1 returns it to the chip (`universe-surface.tsx`), closing map 2 to the chip as well (`use-region-encounter.ts`, guarded by the session still being live). The screen's own focus effect (`universe-screen.tsx`) does nothing in the trial look.
- **What a tap on the flat map chooses.** The drawing has one press layer (`flat-planet-press`, not a tab stop, not named) and no press handler on any shape; `map-hit.ts` (pure) decides from the tap's point. Land under the tap wins; otherwise the nearest formed region whose reach is within 24 points of the tap, where a region's reach is its land or a 44-point target round its point (whichever is nearer), measured in layout points whatever the drawing's size, and seam aware. Land not reached is not an input, so it is never a target, and a tap on it or the sea chooses nothing. A region's own map has no layer.
- **What they draw.** `use-flat-planet.ts` and `use-region-encounter.ts` copy `usePlanetSurface` and the old region card's Open logic on purpose, so the files other work owns stay untouched. They are also seen over labelled sample data at `/gallery-space?scene=maps` (see "Looking at it locally").

## What is kept on the device

Per account and privacy epoch, purged with it: `universe.view.v1` (where you were, Lately, Simple drawing, the last place; `universe-memory.ts`), `universe.here.v1` (`universe-here-marker.ts`), `universe.landmark-seen.v1` (`collection/`) and `universe.crossing-seen.v1` (`features/visit/crossing-seen.ts`: the public handles of the friends whose crossing was shown, newest 200, so the film is 7.5 s the first time and 3.6 s after). The Back history (32 steps) lives in memory. Returning from a Reel or Scroll uses a `scene-return.v1` locator (`universe-camera.ts`), a wire schema shared with the server: what it holds must fit its bounds. Looking at the map writes no evidence and sends no write.

## Speed

The frame loop (`space/space-view.tsx`, `space/render-loop.ts`) draws at most one frame every 33 ms, at a pixel ratio of at most 1.5 (D-073), and stops when the screen is not in front. With "Show the Universe frame rate" on, `frame-rate/` shows the frame rate over the canvas (developer use, removed when the trial ends). A scene rebuilt from scratch frees every shader program and links it again; the trial avoids that during flights (see `apps/client/README.md`, "No stall from a rebuilt scene").

## Looking at it locally

Build the web export and the developer gallery (`pnpm --filter @knowscroll/client build` and `build:gallery`; a new worktree first needs `pnpm --filter "@knowscroll/client^..." build`). The gallery page `/gallery-space?scene=voyage` draws the trial look over labelled sample places, with no account. `/gallery-space?scene=crossing&q=0.30&dir=out&name=Ravi&shared=Attention,Stars` draws one still of the crossing at film time `q` (`&play=1` plays it). `/gallery-space?scene=maps` draws the two maps over labelled sample data: `map=1|2`, `regions=1..12` (how many have formed), `seam=1` (a region across the band's seam; on map 2 it is the one shown), `here=1` ("you were here"), `preview=scroll|reel|loading|offline|unavailable|blocked` (map 2), `hypothetical=1` and `type=lush|ocean|frozen|scorched|barren|gas`; choosing a region on map 1 opens its map and Back returns. The browser specs open `/universe` over authored fixtures (`universe.fixture.ts`, `e2e/voyage-fixture.ts`).

## Tests

- Unit tests sit beside the code (`*.test.ts(x)`, Jest): the session, the layout, the camera maths, the flight, the painter (its pixels are pinned by hash), the driver over a real scene without a GPU.
- Browser specs (`apps/client/e2e/universe-*.spec.ts`, Playwright, headless Chromium with software WebGL) find places by button name, count frames and read pixels from screenshots. They never wait for a moment inside an animation, and assert no frame rate: software WebGL is not a phone.
- `space/fake-space-gpu.ts` stands in for WebGL in screen tests.
