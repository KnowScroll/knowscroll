How it evolves

A planet, fed with curiosity, becomes a sun

Attention is the only input. Watch something inside Technology and a stop lands on one of its continents. Ask about it and another lands. When the stops spread across two of its sub-concepts and there are enough of them, the planet has earned a system: it swells, brightens, and its continents fly out as planets. The star then pulses; its gravity is what keeps the planets in orbit. The same rule runs one level down.

You can do this with the day buttons, or by hand: open the planet and press “Ask about it” until it ignites. Both are the same rule; one is a month, the other is a minute.

day 1One planet

You watched three things about machine learning. Technology is a planet with one continent drawn and the rest under clouds. Nothing else in the sky but a faint friend, later.

day 6It fills

Robotics and compilers appear as continents. A clip about Ghibli became a tiny free planet, far off. The planet is visibly bigger and its glow is warmer.

day 12It ignites

Attention spread across three sub-concepts. Technology becomes a star; machine learning, robotics and compilers take orbits, closest with the most stops. A station appears on the middle orbit.

day 30A system with weather

Machine learning is ready to ignite next. A kept question became a black hole in open space between systems, bending the stars behind it. A nebula sits where you have nothing. Predictive History is a free planet, growing.

The black hole

What it is, how it looks, how gravity shows

A black hole is a question you kept that nobody has answered. It has gravity because you keep coming back to it. It is not inside any system: a question that stays open long enough stops belonging to one concept, so it sits in open space between systems and pulls on all of them. Everything below is built so that it feels like that, not like a hazard.

The rule that keeps it honest: a black hole never appears on its own. It needs a kept question with no answer, and it leaves the moment a room settles the question.

in the prototype

Stars bend around it

The starfield behind the hole is pushed outward into a ring, the way lensing does it. Orbit lines that pass near it bow away. You see the gravity before you see the hole.

in the prototype

A disk that turns, a ring of light

An accretion disk of orange and teal turns around a black centre; a thin bright ring marks the edge nothing comes back from. Three small fragments spiral in: the pieces of the question you keep circling.

in the prototype

The ship’s path bends

Fly between two systems with the hole in the way and the ship curves around it. Gravity is felt in travel, not explained in a label.

in the prototype

It pulls on where it came from

A dotted pull line runs to Technology, the system the question was kept in. The hole sits on no orbit; you reach it by ship, the same way you reach a system.

in the prototype

Answer it and it becomes a star

Take the question to a room. When the room settles it, the hole flashes and collapses into a small star: a question became an idea, and it now lights things instead of pulling them. That is the whole reward, and it is a change in the world, not a score.

Not built: time runs slow near it

Reels you watch near the hole play their captions a beat slower and the progress bar stretches: the question makes you dwell. A small thing, and possibly too clever.

Stations, ships, moons

Places to land, a way to travel, things that circle

in the prototype

The station

A ring on the middle orbit where anyone can land. It belongs to no planet. Inside: the friends who are here (later), the rooms that are arguing, and things people left for you. It is the README’s Station, drawn as a place you dock at rather than a tab you open.

in the prototype

The ship

You are somewhere, always. The ship waits beside the planet you are at; tap another and it flies, then you approach. It is the surveyor from the sea charts, given an engine. Its trail fades; its path curves around gravity.

in the prototype

Moons

Every planet has a moon: your Keep. What you keep on a planet rides with you, and each kept thing is a crater. Go over a planet in the system and its moon shows; on the planet itself it circles in front of the globe, and tapping it lists what you kept. A second, smaller kind is a sub-concept two planets share: Codes circles machine learning and belongs to compilers too.

Three questions: (1) The ship makes travel visible and slow, about a second and a half. Is that the right price for the feeling, or should a second tap skip the flight? (2) Should the station be one per system, or one per universe, parked between systems? (3) Growing a planet by hand with “Ask about it” shows the rule in a minute; in the product, only real watching and asking count. Should the button exist at all, or only in this demo? (4) The moon is drawn as your Keep. Is that the right thing to orbit a planet, or should the Keep stay a tab and the moon stay a shared sub-concept?
The planet, the map

Drawn the way a chart is drawn

The globe and the interior now use one drawing grammar: jagged coasts, three stacked shores from shallow to deep, a sea with a graticule, shelves, currents and wave marks, islets as reefs, cloud banks at the rim, and a compass. Nothing on either screen is a label unless it has to be.

Uncharted land is grey under cloud with a badge and no padlock. It is not locked; nobody has been there yet. A stop landing there, or a probe finding something sound, clears the cloud.

in the prototype

Coasts

Continents are polygons with straight, notched edges, not blobs. Each has a deep shore, a shallow shore and a top, offset like a paper cut-out, so the land reads as land at any zoom. The same shapes sit on the globe, wrapped around it.

in the prototype

Sea

A radial ocean, a faint graticule, a pale shelf around every coast, dashed currents that move slowly, small wave marks, and a compass rose in the corner. The zoom control shows a percentage, like a chart.

in the prototype

Islets and clouds

Each stop is an octagonal islet on a sand bank inside a dashed reef ring, tinted by its continent; new ones ping. Clouds are lobed puffs with a shadow, banked around the rim where there is no land and stacked over uncharted land.
